# Sell Spino as a habit, not a feature

Spino should launch as a **freemium subscription at £4.99/month or £34.99/year**. The free tier should be good enough for the core organising habit, and premium should be anchored on auto-cutting class recaps into 8-count snippets. The bigger long-term bet is a **"coach pays, student free" school plan** that turns Spino Dance Method and partner instructors into the acquisition channel. The consumer app alone will probably be a small business. Under 15% store fees a £4.99 subscription nets about **£3.53/month**. The UK Bachata/Salsa scene is plausibly only in the low tens of thousands of regular dancers (an estimate; no census exists). Freemium apps convert a median of about 2% of downloads. Realistic early consumer revenue is therefore hundreds of pounds a month, not thousands. Differentiation also has to be stated honestly. Auto 8-count detection already ships in DanceBeat, Ritmo and DanceCopilot, and at least eight indie "organise your dance videos" apps launched in 2025–26. None of them combines Bachata position roadmaps, auto-cut filing of class recaps, Android, a UK focus and an instructor channel. That combination, plus the brand's real relationships with students, is the defensible position. The beat-detection algorithm is not. Much of the evidence is thin: dance-specific conversion, churn and seasonality data do not exist publicly, so the benchmarks below are general app-economy proxies and the pricing is reasoned rather than tested.

## A crowded niche of tiny apps, with nobody yet winning

The "my class videos are lost in my camera roll" problem is real, and that is why many indie developers have attacked it. Terpsi (released June 2026) offers non-destructive trimming, frame-by-frame playback, tags and spaced-recall review with local or iCloud storage. It has a free core and a one-time "Plus" unlock, and an estimated **under 1,000 downloads** ([mwm.ai](https://mwm.ai/apps/terpsi-dance-practice-videos/6780994356)). Sweet Dance Moves Library covers salsa and bachata tagging and imports for a **$2.99 lifetime or $9.99/year** premium, and has 2 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6749212354)). Dance Dex auto-organises videos by recognised song, is free and has 0 ratings ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6746681807)). MemoDanse, Let's Dance, DanceVault, DanceLib and Dance Cam fill out the field. Almost all are iOS-only, and none shows meaningful traction. Two readings follow. The job is validated, because so many people independently built for it. But willingness to pay for a pure organiser looks low, and no one has proven a business there.

The auto-count layer is equally contested. DanceBeat extracts a video's audio, detects beats, splits them into measures and offers 8-count navigation, loop, slow-down, mirror and a manual beat-grid editor, sold as a monthly subscription (about US$2.50 in the Colombian store) ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/co/app/dancebeat/id6757979636)). Ritmo, built specifically for Bachata and Salsa, shows an 8-count grid with colour-coded phrase changes. It charges **$4.99/month, $49.99/year or $149.99 lifetime** on top of a small free quota ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760730410)). DanceCopilot puts an 8-count ruler over the user's own videos, with 10 free imports before a one-time purchase ([chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/id6810995273)). All three are new and have negligible ratings. Loop, slow-mo, mirror and frame-scrub are now table stakes, and auto-8-count is a feature, not a moat. Ritmo's price card is the most direct anchor for what a counts feature can charge.

Further out sit content businesses with much higher prices. STEEZY's UK App Store listing shows a £5.99 entry monthly price next to **£59.99–£99.99 annual plans** ([App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/steezy-learn-how-to-dance/id1296001664)). Its revenue estimates disagree by an order of magnitude, from about $6.5M to $19M a year ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/steezy), [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/steezy-revenue)). Bachata and Salsa education is dominated by instructor-owned course sites: The Dance Dojo sells a **$299 lifetime Bachata programme** ([Dance Dojo](https://thedancedojo.com/product/bachata/)), Incognito Dance in London charges **£40 per online course** ([Incognito](https://incognito-dance.teachable.com/p/home)), and Skool communities charge **$27–$50/month** ([Skool](https://www.skool.com/bachatabasics)). Generic sports video-analysis tools have consolidated. Coach's Eye was retired in 2022 ([AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/coach-s-eye/about)), and OnForm, which absorbed Hudl Technique, charges individuals **$9.99/month or $99.99/year** ([OnForm](https://www.onform.com/pricing)).

Mapping the field this way shows where Spino can honestly stand. No product found combines (a) Bachata-specific position vocabulary and roadmaps of positions and transitions, (b) auto-cutting of *class recap* videos into snippets filed straight into those roadmaps, (c) an Android build, (d) a UK focus, and (e) an instructor or school channel. Each element can be copied, Ritmo or DanceBeat could add filing, and Terpsi could add counts. The durable advantage is therefore distribution through the Spino Dance Method brand and partner teachers, not technology. One caveat: Android equivalents were not systematically searched, so the "Android whitespace" claim is an inference from the iOS-heavy sample.

## Dancers spend £50–£250 a month on dance, but pay little for utilities

Spend data supports a ~£5 price comfortably. London drop-in classes run **£12–£20**, socials £10–£18, and City Academy's unlimited salsa and bachata membership costs **£110/month** ([Global Dance Finder](https://global-dance-finder-astro.pages.dev/guides/where-to-dance-bachata-in-the-uk/), [City Academy](https://www.city-academy.com/salsa-subscription)). Private lessons run about £80/hour ([ClassBento](https://classbento.co.uk/bachata-private-dance-course-london)). 2026 festival full passes run from **£75 to €175** ([Tickettailor](https://www.tickettailor.com/events/sueoevents/2278778), [go&dance](https://www.goandance.com/en/event/9503/pulse-bachata-festival-2026)). By my arithmetic, a once-a-week dancer spends about £50–£75 a month, so £4.99 is 7–10% of that. A committed dancer with festivals spends £150–£250 averaged across the year, so £4.99 is 2–3%. "Less than one drop-in class a month to remember everything you learned" is a framing the numbers support. At £8 the app starts competing with a cheap class, which validates the £5 ceiling.

The behaviour Spino depends on is established. Teachers commonly allow or offer filming of the combination at the end of class ([Dance Spirit](https://dancespirit.com/filming-at-conventions/)). One US salsa and bachata school explicitly tells students they have "lots of videos taking up space on our phones doing nothing" and recommends a labelled, dated library ([Movers & Shakers](https://www.moversandshakersdance.com/how-to-learn-faster-in-salsa-class-and-bachata-class/lesson-13-old-vs-new)). Dancers already buy DIY fixes, such as a **$15 Gumroad "Dance Moves Cheat Sheet"** organising moves by name, instructor and venue ([Gumroad](https://bachatabutterfly.gumroad.com/l/moves)). Instructors are turning recaps into products: Tres Golpes posts a weekly class-combo recap on Skool ([Skool](https://www.skool.com/tresgolpes/welcome-to-tres-golpes-bachata)). The audience skews female (about **67–71%** across three academic samples) with a salsa mean age around 33 ([PMC4372397](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4372397), [PMC5267615](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5267615)). Bachata looks like the growth segment, roughly level with salsa in one London weekly listings snapshot (12 vs 13 events) ([Global Dance Finder](https://global-dance-finder-astro.pages.dev/venues/london/)).

The tension is that dancers pay generously for *content and access* ($19–$50/month communities, £110 memberships) but the organiser apps they would compare Spino to sell for $2–$10 a year. The real price competitor is free: the camera roll, Google Photos albums and WhatsApp class groups. Spino's premium therefore has to *do work* the user cannot do for free, which is cutting and filing a 20-minute recap automatically. Making storage or organisation feel scarce would not work.

Three data gaps matter for pricing. No source counts UK Latin dancers; my "low tens of thousands of regulars" estimate is extrapolated from venue listings and capacities. No data exists on what fraction of students film, or on where they currently store videos. And Reddit threads (r/Bachata, r/Salsa) were not retrievable, so qualitative willingness-to-pay evidence is anecdotal.

## Freemium costs conversion but buys the distribution Spino needs

RevenueCat's 2026 data on 115k+ apps is blunt. Hard paywalls convert a median **10.7% of downloads to paid by day 35 versus 2.1% for freemium**, and earn about **8x more revenue per install** at day 60 ($3.09 vs $0.38). One-year retention of annual subscribers is essentially identical (27% vs 28%) ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md)). Taken on its own, that argues for a hard paywall. RevenueCat's own guidance, however, reserves hard paywalls for apps solving acute problems or needing quick cash. It recommends freemium where growth comes from word of mouth, network effects or user-generated content ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/hard-paywall-vs-freemium.md)). Spino is a slow-building habit fed by user-generated video, and its best channel is a teacher telling a whole class to install it. A teacher cannot comfortably send students to an app that charges on first open. Coaching comparators settle this the same way: CoachNow promises athletes "never pay a dime" ([CoachNow](https://coachnow.io/freetrialreferral)), and OnForm athletes pay nothing when linked to a coach ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1490334045)). Freemium is the right call. Spino should claw back conversion with a trial offer and a usage-metered "magic" feature rather than a blunt wall.

Trial length matters more than most indies assume. Trials of **17–32 days convert 42.5%** to paid versus 25.5% for trials of four days or fewer. **55.4% of 3-day-trial cancellations happen on day 0** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md)). For a dance app whose value appears only after a user has filmed a class or two, a long trial fits the weekly class rhythm.

On plan mix, the evidence favours monthly plus annual, with annual as the visual anchor. Offering annual-only usually yields less long-term revenue except in highly seasonal apps ([RevenueCat SOSA 2024](https://www-docs.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2024/)). Annuals are fragile: month 1 accounts for **35% of annual cancellations**, and about 72% of annual subscribers had turned off auto-renew by the end of year one in 2026 ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md)). Lifetime deals are most common in Education and Photo & Video, the categories Spino straddles. They suit apps with near-zero marginal cost, and multiples run from 2x to 12x annual ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions.md)). Their downsides are cannibalising loyal subscribers and blocking future upsells, and a lifetime purchase cannot carry a free trial. Spino's processing cost is under a penny per video, so lifetime carries little cost risk. It works best as a tactical offer (founders, festival, win-back) rather than a permanent paywall option, the way Fiit sends lifetime offers as email win-backs ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/lifetime-subscriptions.md)).

B2B2C pricing in adjacent sports and coaching markets clusters around charging the coach, not the student. CoachNow+ costs about **$24.99 per coach per month** ([G2](https://g2.com/products/coachnow/pricing)), OnForm coach plans cost **$19.99–$59.99/month** with unlimited athletes ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1490334045)), and Hudl club plans run **$400–$1,600 per team per year** ([Hudl](https://www.hudl.com/pricing/club/basketball)). Dance studios already pay $49–$245/month for management software, with a branded student app as an upsell ([Gymdesk](https://gymdesk.com/blog/best-dance-software)). No dance-specific B2B2C recap tool was found. That is either whitespace or a sign that studios won't pay; it is unproven either way. For content, splits depend on who brings the audience. BJJ Fanatics reportedly pays about 50/50 ([BJJMore](https://bjjmore.com/bjj-net-worth/)), and Udemy pays 97% on instructor-promoted sales versus 37% on marketplace sales ([Ruzuku](https://ruzuku.com/learn/articles/is-udemy-any-good)). The festival scene runs on instructor discount codes and street teams ([NYISC](https://www.newyorksalsacongress.com/street), [Hawaii Congress](https://www.eventcreate.com/e/hawaiisalsabachatacongress)). No documented case exists of a dance app bundled with congress passes, so festival bundles are a hypothesis to test, not a proven channel.

## The store takes about 29% of a £4.99 subscription, and 1,000 installs earn about £70 a month

Android-first is cheap to run. Since **30 June 2026**, auto-renewing subscriptions on Google Play in the UK, US and EEA pay a **10% service fee plus a 5% billing fee** via Play Billing, 15% in all. Alternative billing or a web link-out drops the 5% billing fee but adds the processor's costs ([Google Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622?hl=en)). One-time purchases cost more: 15–20% plus 5% on new installs. A lifetime unlock on Play therefore loses up to about a quarter to fees, against 15% for a subscription. On iOS later, the Small Business Program rate is 15% ([Funnelfox](https://blog.funnelfox.com/apple-app-store-fees-2026-eu-dma/)). The UK CMA's steering rules, which could allow cheaper link-outs, were still pending as of November 2026 ([CMA consultation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a42a56a3413112faed80e26/Consultation_document__Apple_steering_.pdf)). Apple figures come from a vendor blog and should be verified on developer.apple.com. Stores remit UK VAT and pay out net. Google applies VAT to the full amount and Apple applies it post-commission, though proceeds come out the same ([RevenueCat docs](https://revenuecat.com/docs/dashboard-and-metrics/taxes-and-commissions)). RevenueCat costs nothing until **$2,500 monthly tracked revenue** ([RevenueCat pricing](https://revenuecat.com/pricing)), roughly 700 monthly subscribers at £4.99, so Spino will sit in the free tier for a long time.

| Price (incl. 20% VAT) | Net at 15% fee (subscription) | Net at 20% fee (Play one-time) |
|---|---|---|
| £3.99/month | £2.83 | — |
| **£4.99/month** | **£3.53** | — |
| £29.99/year | £21.24 | — |
| **£34.99/year** | **£24.78** | — |

| £89.99 lifetime | — | £59.99 |

*My arithmetic: price ÷ 1.2, minus fee. Not an official proceeds table.*

Benchmarks frame the ambition. Median category subscription prices are **$9.99/month and $44.99/year for Education**, and $9.99 and about $39.94 for Health & Fitness ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)), so a £4.99 ceiling sits well below the median. Download-to-trial runs about **6.5–6.9%** in those categories. Western Europe converts download-to-paid at about **2.0%** and trial-to-paid at 29.7%. Median day-60 revenue per install is $0.33 in Europe. Only **17.3% of new apps reach $1k monthly revenue within two years** ([SOSA](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps)). Applied to Spino, 1,000 installs at 2% paid gives about 20 subscribers, or about **£70 MRR**. If 10% of a hypothetical 20,000 regular UK dancers installed, that is 2,000 installs and about £140 MRR from consumers. These are illustrative calculations stacked on an estimated market size, and they show why school deals and the funnel value to Spino Dance Method matter. One Android-specific hazard deserves engineering attention: billing failures cause **31% of Google Play cancellations versus 14% on the App Store** ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md)). Grace periods and account hold should be enabled from day one.

## Recommendation: £4.99 / £34.99, auto-cut as the paywall, schools as the channel

### Pricing tiers (GBP, UK storefront)

| Tier | Price | Who it's for | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | £0 | Every student a teacher sends | Core organising habit stays free forever |
| Premium monthly | **£4.99/month** | Trialists, seasonal dancers | Sits at the ceiling. Discount via codes, never via a low list price |
| Premium annual | **£34.99/year** (≈42% saving; ~£2.92/month) | Committed dancers | Default-selected anchor on the paywall; nets ~£24.78 |
| Term pass (phase 3) | £12.99 for 4 months, non-renewing | Students on a term course | Matches September/January course intakes; test before committing |
| Lifetime (tactical only) | **£89.99** (~2.6x annual) | Founders, festival, win-back | Not on the default paywall; time-boxed offers only |
| School / instructor plan (phase 3) | **£15–£30/month per instructor** or ~£150–£300/year per school | Teachers and schools | Unlocks Premium for linked students. Indicative prices, modelled on CoachNow and OnForm; must be validated |

The £34.99 annual price undercuts Ritmo ($49.99) and the Education median ($44.99) while staying well above indie organisers ($9.99). The gap is justified only if auto-cut works reliably on real Bachata class audio. If it does not, drop to £24.99–£29.99 rather than cutting the monthly price. Starting near the ceiling and discounting through codes keeps headroom, while launching at £1.99 makes later rises painful. All tier prices are my reasoning from the benchmarks above; none has been tested on dancers.

### Free vs paid split

Free should include unlimited importing of on-phone videos, manual snippet cutting, up to **two roadmaps** (enough for one style and one practice list), the Bachata position vocabulary, and a **small monthly auto-cut quota (three recaps)** so every user experiences the magic. Premium should unlock unlimited roadmaps, unlimited auto-cut of class and festival recaps, Spino Method roadmap templates, and convenience features such as export/share and roadmap backup. The logic is to gate *volume of the magic*, not the habit. A user who films one class a week hits the quota in their first month, at exactly the moment they have proof the feature saves time. Offer a **30-day Premium trial** when the user first hits a quota (trials of 17–32 days convert best), not on first launch. Treat the gate thresholds as starting points for A/B testing. No source gives dance-specific evidence on which caps convert.

### Phased plan

**Pre-launch (now → Play launch).** Recruit 30–100 Spino Dance Method students as a closed Play testing group. Measure two things above all: whether auto-cut produces correct 8-counts on real class recaps, and how many recaps a typical student films per week. In RevenueCat, set up the monthly, annual and lifetime products plus a "founders" Play promo code. Turn on grace period and account hold. Run a simple price-intent survey (Van Westendorp-style) with the test group, since no market data covers this audience. Pre-sell a limited founders lifetime at £59.99 to the brand's own students to test real willingness to pay and raise early cash.

**Launch (Android, UK).** Launch freemium with the quota-triggered 30-day trial and the £4.99/£34.99 paywall, annual pre-selected. Optimise onboarding to reach the first roadmap and first auto-cut in the first session, since day 0 decides trials. Seed through Spino Dance Method classes and socials with the "less than one class a month" message. Track download-to-trial against the ~6.5% benchmark and trial-to-paid against ~30% (Western Europe). Below roughly half of those, fix the product before adding channels.

**Growth (months 4–18).** Pilot the instructor plan free with three to five partner schools, then convert pilots to paid. Distribute student Premium through Play promo codes (up to **10,000 one-time codes per quarter per subscription** ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/6321495?hl=en))) or through server-side entitlements for directly invoiced licences, after checking store policy. Ship iOS, where competitors are concentrated, only once Android retention is proven. Add Salsa vocabulary. Test a festival bundle ("full pass + 3 months Premium" via offer codes) with one UK festival such as Pulse or Ritmo de Bachata, and an instructor ambassador code paying 20–30% of first-year net (an unbenchmarked figure). Release first-party Spino Method content packs as roadmap templates with reference clips. Defer a third-party marketplace until volume justifies payouts and contracts. Consider purchasing-power pricing at 40–60% of the UK price for LatAm and Asian storefronts, and web checkout for annual plans only once revenue is meaningful. On a £4.99 monthly plan, Stripe's fixed fee wipes out most of the 5-point saving ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/google-play-billing-changes-subscriptions-fees/)).

### Risks to manage

The largest risk is that **willingness to pay is lower than the £5 ceiling implies**, because the visible anchors are $2.99 lifetime organisers and the free camera roll. Mitigate by gating auto-cut volume rather than storage, and keep the annual price flexible. Second, **differentiation is fragile**: Ritmo, DanceBeat or Terpsi could add roadmap filing or Android within months, so speed to schools and brand relationships matter more than features. Third, **auto-cut accuracy on noisy class audio is unproven**. No competitor publishes bachata accuracy, and a mis-cut snippet destroys trust in the premium feature. Fourth, **brand conflict**: a heavily Spino-branded app may put off rival schools. Keep the consumer app neutral in feel, with Spino Method as a content layer and co-branded school roadmaps. Fifth, **seasonality and churn**: Health & Fitness proxies show January spikes and February–March churn ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/how-to-tackle-new-year-subscription-churn.md)), dance probably follows term and festival calendars instead, and Play's involuntary churn is double iOS's. Sixth, **policy risk** on school licences sold outside the stores and on promo-code eligibility for under-age students, which was not researched. Finally, a **small total market**: the illustrative maths caps UK consumer revenue in the low hundreds of pounds a month in year one, so success depends on schools, international expansion and the funnel value to Spino Dance Method's classes and courses.

## Conclusion

Spino competes with the camera roll and a dozen hobbyist apps, not with STEEZY. It wins by being the tool a teacher hands out on day one, not the cleverest beat detector. That shifts the monetisation question away from the exact consumer price point. £4.99/£34.99 is defensible and below every category median, but consumer revenue will be modest whatever the price. The more important question is whether instructors will pay, or at least distribute, to make their students practise. The most valuable pre-launch experiment is therefore not a paywall test. It is putting auto-cut in front of real students after real classes and watching whether they come back next week. If they do, that retention evidence is what sells the school plan.

The research also reframes Spino's relationship to its parent brand. If app revenue stays small, its strategic value lies in what it does for Spino Dance Method: retaining students between classes, giving the brand a reason to be in their pocket daily, and creating a product partner schools can adopt. That argues for measuring success at launch in weekly active roadmaps and school pilots, with MRR as a lagging indicator. Several inputs are still unknown: UK dancer counts, filming rates, dance-specific conversion and churn, Apple's verified UK fee terms and the CMA outcome. Treat every number here as a starting hypothesis to replace with Spino's own RevenueCat data within the first 90 days.
